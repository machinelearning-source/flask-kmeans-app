Part 2: Clustering Application (Scikit-Learn)

K-Means applied to 1,000 flight records with the distance and duration variables.

Dataset justification and preprocessing

We selected 1,000 flights with two fully numerical variables:

  • distance_km (range {{ feature_ranges.distance[0] }} – {{ feature_ranges.distance[1] }})
  • duration_min (range {{ feature_ranges.duration[0] }} – {{ feature_ranges.duration[1] }})

Why these variables? Both capture the "size" of a flight directly, they are numeric (no categorical encoding needed), and they correlate strongly with operational costs and crew scheduling. Preprocessing: missing values were removed and the variables were standardized (mean 0, standard deviation 1) so that the Euclidean distance is not dominated by the larger-magnitude variable.

This value is above 0 and close to acceptable levels, which indicates that the records are reasonably compact inside their own cluster and reasonably separated from the other clusters. A score near 1 would mean perfectly separated clusters; near 0 would mean overlapping clusters.

Interpretation of the clusters

Cluster 0 corresponds to short-range regional flights, operated on local routes. Cluster 1 groups mid-range flights used in national/domestic networks. Cluster 2 contains long-range flights typical of international operations, with the highest fuel consumption and longest crew duty times. These segments are useful for fleet assignment, fuel planning, and ticket pricing.
